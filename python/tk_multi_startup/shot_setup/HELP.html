Shot Setup

MAYA · VFXIM PIPELINE

Shot Setup gets a shot ready the first time you open it: a shot camera with the right lens, the plate on an image plane, and the right resolution and frame range. You don't have to do anything for it.

What happens automatically

When you open a shot through ShotGrid > File Open and it's a new, unsaved scene (File Open > + New File), Shot Setup creates:

shotCam_<seq>_<shot>The shot camera. Focal length from the shot on ShotGrid, filmback (sensor size) from the camera linked to the shot. You look through it straight away.
plate_<seq>_<shot>An image plane on that camera with the plate: the newest plate version, EXR first. Frame 1001 in Maya shows plate frame 1001.
SceneRender resolution and pixel aspect of the plate, and the shot's frame range.

It happens once per scene; a small message at the top of the viewport tells you it ran. Ctrl+Z takes it all back in one step. Opening an existing work file never changes it by itself. Only when that file is still completely empty, Shot Setup asks once whether you want the camera and plate; it doesn't ask again for that file.

Shot Setup window

ShotGrid > Shot Setup... shows what ShotGrid and the project folder say about the shot, next to what's in your scene:

OKThe scene matches.
UPDATESomething changed, e.g. a new focal length on ShotGrid or a newer plate version. Press the Update button of that block.
MISSINGNot in the scene yet (for example an older scene). Set up shot adds it.

Updates only change the lens (focal length, filmback) and the image plane's file and frame offset. Your camera animation and the image plane's depth, transparency and placement stay as they are. Pick another plate version in the Plate list if you need an older or a different one.

Where the information comes from

Focal length – the shot's Focal Length field on ShotGrid (mm). Empty: 35 mm.
Filmback – the width from the shot's Camera on ShotGrid (Filmback Width, mm; no camera linked: 36 mm). The height follows the plate's aspect, so the film gate matches the plate.
Plate – first the footage folder chosen in Shot Manager, then 02_source/sc<seq>/<shot>/p###/plates/, then plates published to the shot on ShotGrid.
Frame range – the shot's Cut In / Cut Out, otherwise the plate's frames.

For production: setting up a camera on ShotGrid

  1. In the project, open the Cameras page (not in the navigation? Project Actions > Tracking Settings, set Camera to visible).
  2. + Camera: a name (e.g. Sony Venice 6K 3:2) and Filmback Width / Height in mm.
  3. On the Shots page, add the Camera and Focal Length columns and fill them in. Select several shots to fill them in at once.

Then press Reload and Update camera in the Shot Setup window of scenes that already exist.

Turning the automatic part off

For a whole config: shot_setup_auto: false under tk-multi-startup. On one machine: environment variable NFA_SHOT_SETUP_AUTO=0. The menu item keeps working.
